# SecureCall / Stealth — Brand & Messaging Guidelines (v1.0, Draft)

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how we **talk about** SecureCall / Stealth:

- in the repository (README, docs),
- on websites and landing pages,
- in pitch decks and investor material,
- in press or public communication.

The goal is:

- to position SecureCall clearly as a **legitimate, privacy-preserving security product**,  
- and to avoid language that could associate the project with criminal “crypto phones” (SkyECC, Encrochat, An0m, …).

---

## 2. Core positioning

SecureCall / Stealth should be consistently presented as:

- a **secure, end-to-end encrypted voice calling system**,
- built for **journalists, NGOs, companies, activists and private individuals**,
- focusing on **privacy, human rights and information security**,
- implemented as **open-source software** with transparent cryptography,
- deployed on a **hardened Android-based OS / ROM profile**.

We are **not** building:

- a “black box” underground communication system,
- a service for organised crime,
- a tool to “beat the police”.

---

## 3. Primary audiences

When writing or speaking about SecureCall, keep these audiences in mind:

1. **Journalists and human rights defenders**
   - Need: protect sources, avoid surveillance, stay safe in hostile environments.
2. **NGOs and civil society**
   - Need: secure coordination, privacy for staff and partners.
3. **Companies and professionals**
   - Need: confidential calls (legal, finance, R&D, executives).
4. **Privacy-conscious individuals**
   - Need: modern, private communication without Big-Tech profiling.
5. **Security researchers and open-source community**
   - Need: clear security model, open code, honest documentation.

---

## 4. Language to prefer (“Do”)

Use language that emphasises:

- **privacy & security**
  - “privacy by design”
  - “end-to-end encrypted voice calls”
  - “minimal metadata”
  - “user-controlled keys”
- **human rights & democracy**
  - “protecting journalists and sources”
  - “supporting free press and civil society”
- **transparency & openness**
  - “open source”
  - “auditable cryptography”
  - “no hidden backdoors”
- **professionalism**
  - “for NGOs, companies and professionals”
  - “security-focused Android profile”
  - “robust threat model”

Example phrases:

- “SecureCall provides end-to-end encrypted voice communication for people who need real privacy.”
- “The system is open source, audit-friendly and designed around data minimisation.”
- “It is meant for journalists, NGOs, companies and privacy-conscious individuals.”

---

## 5. Language to avoid (“Don’t”)

Avoid language that suggests:

- immunity from law enforcement,
- use for crime,
- invisibility or undetectability.

**Never** use phrases like:

- “police-proof”
- “untraceable” / “undetectable”
- “perfect anonymity”
- “avoid law enforcement”
- “forbidden communications”
- “bulletproof against authorities”
- “like Encrochat / SkyECC, but better”

Also avoid:

- references to “cartels”, “mafias”, “darknet vendors”,
- marketing comparisons to known criminal “crypto phones”.

Even if technically tempting, these terms:

- attract the wrong audience,
- create reputational and legal risk,
- contradict the project’s values and governance.

---

## 6. Tone & style

### 6.1 Serious and credible

- Write in a **calm, professional tone**.
- Avoid over-hype and buzzwords.
- Be honest about limitations (no magic guarantees, no “unbreakable forever”).

### 6.2 Technically accurate

- Do not promise more than the system can technically deliver.
- If something is “planned” or “experimental”, say so.
- Distinguish clearly between:
  - what exists today,
  - what is on the roadmap,
  - what is long-term vision.

### 6.3 Rights-focused, not anti-state

- Emphasise protection of individuals and organisations against **illegal or mass surveillance**.
- Do not use anti-police or anti-state rhetoric.
- Make clear:
  - The project does not obstruct legal processes.
  - It simply does not possess decryption keys or centralised data.

---

## 7. Concrete guidance for common artifacts

### 7.1 GitHub README

The README should:

- describe SecureCall as a **secure voice system for high-risk users and organisations**,
- link to:
  - `docs/legal/LEGAL_POSITIONING.md`
  - `docs/privacy/LOGGING_POLICY.md`
  - `docs/risk/FEATURE_RISK_REGISTER.md`
- **not**:
  - brag about being “undetectable”,
  - mention criminal use cases,
  - contain provocative law-enforcement language.

### 7.2 Website / landing page

A typical home page may emphasise:

- use cases: journalism, NGOs, corporate, privacy enthusiasts,
- technical aspects: E2EE, minimal metadata, hardened OS,
- trust factors: open source, security reviews, transparent design.

Avoid:

- “no one can ever trace you”,
- “designed to beat surveillance agencies”,
- or images/graphics that suggest criminal underground usage.

### 7.3 Pitch decks / investor material

Focus on:

- market need for secure communications,
- regulatory acceptance of E2EE,
- differentiation via OS hardening and privacy-by-design,
- open-source trustworthiness.

Avoid:

- edgy “spy gadget” aesthetic,
- glorifying state confrontation,
- anything that looks like “Encrochat 2.0”.

---

## 8. Handling press and public questions

If journalists or partners ask:

- “Is this like SkyECC / Encrochat?”  
  → answer along the lines of:

  > “No. Those were closed, proprietary systems marketed into criminal ecosystems.  
  > SecureCall is open source, transparent, and aimed at journalists, NGOs, companies and citizens who need privacy and security.”

- “Can criminals use it?”  
  → answer:

  > “As with any encryption technology, misuse is possible.  
  > But SecureCall is explicitly designed, documented and governed as a legitimate security tool, not as a ‘black box’ criminal service.”

---

## 9. Evolution of this document

This document is a **living guideline**.

- As the product, markets and legal environment evolve, messaging may need to be updated.
- Significant changes to positioning should be:
  - discussed with legal/compliance,
  - reflected in `LEGAL_POSITIONING.md`,
  - and communicated to all content creators.

---

## 10. Summary

When in doubt, always ask:

> “If someone who knows nothing about SecureCall reads this text —  
> will they think of Signal / Proton / GrapheneOS,  
> or will they think of Encrochat / SkyECC?”

We always want the answer to be:  
**“Signal / Proton / GrapheneOS.”**
